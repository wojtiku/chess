# Does release 1 serve children?

Type: grilling
Status: resolved

## Question

This was fog until the licensing research found that at least one major provider's terms prohibit
under-18 audiences outright, and require paid-only service in the EEA, UK and Switzerland. The
learner profile is therefore no longer only a pedagogical question — it is a constraint imposed by
whichever provider the coach calls.

Decide: whether release 1 states an adult-only audience, or whether supporting children is a
requirement that pushes us to a different provider or to a different architecture. Note what
"adult-only" costs: the app is then unusable for the population the ChessKid curriculum in the
curriculum research was written for, and the reading level is chosen once and for all.

This was originally a disclosure question ("what do we tell the user?") and is now a scoping
question. It may redraw the destination.

## Answer

**Closed as void: the premise was a provider's terms, and the provider is swappable.**

This ticket was cut when a research finding said one major provider prohibits under-18 audiences
outright and requires paid-only service in the EEA, UK and Switzerland. That made the learner
profile look like a constraint imposed on us. It is not: if a provider's audience rules become a
problem, the answer is to change provider, so no ticket may treat a provider's current terms as a
constraint on the design. Now recorded as a standing constraint in the map so it is not re-derived.

**What survives is a smaller, real question, and it belongs to
[*the learner's path*](13-the-learners-path.md):** what reading level and vocabulary release 1
writes at, given that the first learner is an adult. It is not an eligibility question and it
never was — the original fog entry called it "unasked and still open", which was accurate.

Not decided here, and deliberately: whether release 1 should *also* serve children is a product
preference about the audience, not a blocker. If it is wanted, the reading level and the glossary
both change, and that is work to schedule rather than a reason to stop.
